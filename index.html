About

I started in analytics chasing one question: how do you turn messy data into a decision someone can actually act on? That question carried me from early analyst work into owning the full stack behind an ecommerce business — forecasting, experimentation, dashboards, and lately AI agents that put answers directly in people's hands.

What pulls at me is the point where data meets a real choice. I like owning a problem the whole way through: framing what is worth solving, building the thing, and staying close enough to the outcome to know whether it worked. I am now looking to do that at greater scale and closer to product, where the questions are bigger and the impact compounds.

Amazon market intelligence dashboard

A decision tool for the category managers who set pricing and reorder calls on Amazon — built to replace scattered tabs and gut feel with one shared view.

THE USER

Category managers deciding what to price, promote, and reorder, with no single place to see where a product stood against its competitors.

THE TRADEOFF

Instead of tracking every possible metric, I scoped to the few signals that actually move a decision: competitive price, BSR to sales, review sentiment, and true margin.

THE OUTCOME

Pricing and inventory calls now start from one dashboard and shared evidence rather than separate spreadsheets.

What I weighed. Off the shelf tools existed but were costly, generic, and could not fold in our own margin math. Building it myself meant I could bake in true margin contribution, subtracting creator commissions from reported figures, so a number on the screen mapped to a real decision. I shipped locally on Streamlit first to move fast and sidestep hosting and permission concerns, keeping cloud hosting as a later step once the tool earned it.

What's next: AWS hosting · price and rank alerting · expanding past kitchen scales into adjacent categories
